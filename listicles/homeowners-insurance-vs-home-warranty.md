# Homeowners Insurance vs. a Home Warranty

Homeowners insurance and a home warranty both offer forms of financial protection for homeowners, but they are designed for different situations. Understanding what each one does can help you decide how to protect your home when unexpected problems occur.

## Is a Home Warranty the Same as Home Insurance?

No. Homeowners insurance and home warranties are separate products with different purposes.

Homeowners insurance generally helps protect against covered losses caused by events such as fires, theft, storms, and certain types of property damage. Mortgage lenders also commonly require homeowners insurance.

A home warranty is typically optional. It can help with eligible repair or replacement costs when covered home systems or appliances break down from normal wear and tear.

## What Does a Home Warranty Cover That Homeowners Insurance May Not?

Home warranties are designed to help when covered systems and appliances stop working through regular use.

Depending on the applicable APHW plan and service agreement, coverage may include items such as:

- Central air conditioning and heating systems
- Water heaters
- Plumbing systems
- Electrical systems
- Refrigerators
- Ovens
- Dishwashers
- Built-in microwaves
- Washers and dryers
- Garbage disposals
- Eligible roof leak repairs

For example, homeowners insurance generally would not cover a furnace simply because it stopped working from normal wear and tear. A home warranty may provide assistance with an eligible mechanical breakdown, subject to the terms of the warranty agreement.

## Do I Need a Home Warranty if I Have Homeowners Insurance?

Many homeowners choose to have both because homeowners insurance and home warranties are intended to address different types of problems.

Homeowners insurance may provide protection when a covered event such as a storm, fire, falling tree, theft, or vandalism damages your property.

A home warranty may help when eligible home systems or appliances break down from normal use over time.

For example:

- **Storm damage to a roof:** Homeowners insurance may apply.
- **An eligible roof leak related to normal wear:** A home warranty may provide coverage.
- **Water damage caused by a burst pipe:** Homeowners insurance may help with resulting covered damage.
- **An eligible plumbing stoppage:** A home warranty may help with covered repair costs.

Having both can provide protection for a broader range of circumstances because each product serves a different purpose.
