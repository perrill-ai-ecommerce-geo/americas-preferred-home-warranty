# When Is the Best Time to Buy a Home Warranty?

Unexpected home repairs can happen at inconvenient times. A water heater may stop working shortly after move-in. A dishwasher may break when the household budget is already stretched. An air conditioning system may fail during a hot summer week.

That is why many homeowners ask an important question: **When is the best time to buy a home warranty?**

The best time to buy a home warranty depends on the homeowner’s situation, the condition of the home, and the level of protection they want for covered repair costs. A home warranty can be especially useful when buying a house, selling a house, managing older appliances or home systems, planning around a limited emergency fund, or reviewing coverage after a renovation.

A home warranty is not the same as homeowners insurance. It also does not replace routine maintenance, a home inspection, or emergency savings. Instead, it can provide an added layer of support when covered systems or appliances break down due to normal wear and tear, subject to the warranty agreement.

## 1. When Buying a House

One of the most common times to buy a home warranty is during a real estate transaction.

Even if the home inspection does not uncover major issues, an inspection cannot predict exactly when a working system or appliance may break down in the future. A refrigerator, dishwasher, heating system, air conditioning system, or water heater may operate normally during the inspection but still experience a covered breakdown after the buyer moves in.

For new homeowners, a home warranty can provide a process to follow when a covered item stops working after coverage begins. This can be especially helpful during the first year of homeownership, when buyers may already be managing mortgage payments, moving expenses, furniture purchases, utility setup, and other costs.

In some transactions, the buyer purchases the home warranty. In others, the seller may include one as part of the sale. Either way, a home warranty can help buyers feel more prepared for unexpected covered repairs after closing.

## 2. When Selling a House

A home warranty can also be useful when selling a home.

Sellers may choose to offer a home warranty to make the listing feel more complete and reassuring to buyers. This can be especially valuable when the home has major systems or appliances that are working properly but are no longer brand new.

A common buyer concern is, “What happens if something breaks after I move in?” A home warranty does not guarantee every repair will be covered, but it can give buyers a clear next step if a covered breakdown occurs after closing.

For sellers, offering a home warranty may help strengthen the listing and support buyer confidence. For buyers, it can provide added peace of mind as they transition into the home.

## 3. When Major Appliances or Home Systems Are Getting Older

Another good time to consider a home warranty is when a home’s major systems or appliances are aging.

Many homeowners rely on the same refrigerator, oven, dishwasher, water heater, heating system, air conditioning system, washer, dryer, plumbing, or electrical components for years. Even with regular maintenance, these items can eventually wear down.

A home warranty may help with certain covered repair or replacement costs when covered items break down due to normal wear and tear. This can be helpful for homeowners whose systems and appliances are still functioning but may be more likely to need service over time.

Homeowners should keep expectations clear. A home warranty does not replace regular maintenance. Homeowners still need to care for their systems and appliances, follow manufacturer recommendations, replace filters, clean components, and address issues promptly. Home warranties also generally do not cover known pre-existing conditions.

Think of a home warranty as an added layer of support for covered breakdowns, not a replacement for responsible home maintenance.

## 4. When Emergency Funds Are Low

Home repairs can put pressure on a household budget. Even one unexpected repair may create stress if a homeowner’s emergency fund is already limited.

A home warranty can help homeowners plan for certain covered repair situations by providing a coverage process and service fee structure. Instead of facing the full cost of every covered breakdown alone, homeowners can follow the warranty process outlined in their agreement.

This may be especially useful for:

- First-time homeowners
- Families managing multiple household expenses
- Homeowners with older systems or appliances
- Buyers who recently used savings for a down payment or closing costs
- Homeowners who want more predictability around covered repair costs

A home warranty will not eliminate every expense. Homeowners may still be responsible for service fees, non-covered repairs, upgrades, code-related costs, or expenses that exceed coverage limits. However, coverage may help make certain covered repair situations more manageable.

## 5. After a Renovation or Major Home Update

A renovation can be another good time to review home warranty coverage.

After improving a home, many homeowners want to protect the comfort and function of the updated space. Depending on the project, the renovation may include new appliances, upgraded systems, or improvements that make the homeowner more aware of how important major home components are.

A home warranty may be worth considering after a renovation, especially if some parts of the home were updated while other major systems or appliances remain older. For example, a homeowner may remodel the kitchen but still have an older HVAC system, water heater, or laundry appliance.

Before purchasing coverage, homeowners should review the warranty terms carefully. Some new appliances or systems may already have manufacturer warranties or installer warranties. A home warranty can still be useful, but homeowners should understand how different types of coverage may work together.

## 6. Before a Covered Breakdown Happens

The best time to review home warranty options is before something breaks.

If a system or appliance has already failed, the issue may be considered known or pre-existing, which can affect coverage eligibility. Reviewing coverage while systems and appliances are still working gives homeowners more time to compare options, understand the agreement, and choose a plan that fits their home.

Homeowners should review:

- Covered systems and appliances
- Optional add-ons
- Coverage limits
- Service fees
- Exclusions
- Claim requirements
- Coverage start date
- Contractor requirements
- Renewal options

Understanding these details before a repair is needed can help homeowners feel more confident when a covered breakdown occurs.
