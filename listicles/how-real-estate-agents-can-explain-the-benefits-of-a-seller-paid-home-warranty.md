# How Real Estate Agents Can Explain the Benefits of a Seller-Paid Home Warranty

When a seller lists a home, they are not just selling the property. They are also trying to give prospective buyers confidence in their purchase.

Buyers may have questions about the age and condition of major home systems and appliances. Sellers want to avoid unnecessary surprises during the transaction, while real estate agents want to help both sides move confidently toward closing.

A seller-paid home warranty can be a useful tool during this process. It can add value to a listing, help address buyer concerns about unexpected repairs, and provide the new homeowner with additional protection after closing.

A home warranty does not replace a home inspection, homeowners insurance, or a seller’s responsibility to disclose known issues. Coverage also depends on the applicable warranty agreement, including exclusions, limits, service fees, and claim requirements. However, when positioned accurately, a seller-paid home warranty can provide an additional layer of value during a real estate transaction.

## How Does a Home Warranty Benefit the Seller?

A home warranty can be included as part of a listing or real estate transaction. Depending on the provider and plan, coverage may be available during the listing period, after the sale, or both.

For sellers, the potential benefit extends beyond the warranty coverage itself. Offering a home warranty can give agents another way to strengthen the listing, address common buyer concerns, and demonstrate that the seller is thinking about the buyer’s experience after closing.

Here are five benefits agents can explain to sellers.

## 1. It Can Help Strengthen the Listing

A seller-paid home warranty gives real estate agents another feature to highlight when marketing a property.

This may be especially helpful when a home has older but functioning systems or appliances. Buyers often pay close attention to major items such as:

- HVAC systems
- Plumbing systems
- Electrical systems
- Water heaters
- Refrigerators
- Dishwashers
- Ovens and ranges
- Washers and dryers

Even when these systems and appliances are currently working, buyers may be concerned about the possibility of an unexpected breakdown after moving in.

Including a home warranty gives the agent a simple benefit to communicate: the seller is providing warranty coverage that may help with eligible breakdowns after closing.

## 2. It May Help Address Buyer Concerns About Unexpected Repairs

Buying a home comes with significant upfront expenses. After making a down payment, paying closing costs, moving, and purchasing other necessities, many buyers do not want to face an unexpected repair bill immediately after closing.

A seller-paid home warranty may provide additional peace of mind by giving the buyer a process to follow when an eligible covered system or appliance breaks down.

Agents should avoid presenting a home warranty as a guarantee that nothing will go wrong or that every repair will be covered. Instead, position it as an additional resource that can help buyers prepare for certain covered breakdowns.

That distinction helps set accurate expectations while still communicating the value of the warranty.

## 3. It Can Provide Another Tool During Negotiations

A home inspection can uncover concerns that become part of negotiations between buyers and sellers.

Buyers may ask the seller to:

- Complete repairs
- Replace systems or appliances
- Provide a credit
- Reduce the purchase price
- Address specific inspection findings

Not every older system or appliance necessarily needs immediate replacement. When an item is still functioning but its age makes the buyer nervous, a home warranty may be another option to discuss.

A seller-paid warranty should not be used to conceal known problems or replace necessary disclosures and repairs. However, it may help provide buyers with additional confidence regarding functioning systems or appliances that could experience a future covered breakdown.

## 4. It Can Help Create a Smoother Post-Closing Experience

For sellers, closing usually represents the end of their responsibility for the property. For buyers, it represents the beginning of homeownership.

If an appliance or major system stops working shortly after the transaction, the buyer may instinctively contact their real estate agent or even the seller.

A home warranty provides another path. If the issue may be covered, the homeowner can start a home warranty claim and follow the provider’s process for diagnosing the problem and determining coverage.

That can benefit real estate professionals as well. Giving buyers a clear resource for eligible post-closing breakdowns can support a smoother transition into homeownership and help agents maintain positive client relationships after the sale.

## 5. It Shows the Seller Is Thinking About the Buyer

Including a home warranty can send a positive message.

It demonstrates that the seller is willing to provide something designed to support the buyer after the transaction is complete.

Depending on the market, a seller-paid warranty may also give agents another way to differentiate the property. In a competitive market, it can be an additional listing benefit. In a slower market, it can provide another reason for a buyer to feel comfortable moving forward.

Agents should frame the warranty as added value rather than a promise that all future problems will be covered.

## How Should Real Estate Agents Explain a Seller-Paid Home Warranty?

Keeping the explanation simple can help buyers and sellers understand the role of a home warranty.

Agents can explain that:

- A home warranty is different from homeowners insurance.
- A home warranty does not replace a home inspection.
- Coverage applies according to the specific warranty agreement.
- Not every breakdown or repair is necessarily covered.
- Coverage limits, exclusions, service fees, and claim requirements may apply.
- A seller-paid warranty can provide buyers with additional protection for eligible covered systems and appliances after closing.

Agents should encourage buyers and sellers to review the actual warranty agreement rather than relying solely on a general description of coverage.

## Should Sellers Include a Home Warranty When Listing a Home?

A seller-paid home warranty can be worth considering for many properties, particularly when a home has older but functioning systems or appliances that could raise questions from prospective buyers.

For sellers, a warranty can add another benefit to the listing without making promises about the future condition of the property.

For buyers, it can provide additional confidence and a process to follow if an eligible covered item experiences a breakdown.

For agents, it can serve as a practical tool for marketing the listing, addressing buyer concerns, facilitating conversations during negotiations, and supporting clients after closing.

The right approach depends on the property, the transaction, the seller’s goals, and the specific warranty being offered.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do real estate agents recommend home warranties?

Real estate agents may recommend home warranties because they can add value to a transaction and help buyers feel more prepared for eligible system or appliance breakdowns after closing. They can also give sellers another benefit to include with the property.

### How can a home warranty help strengthen a listing?

A seller-paid home warranty gives agents another feature to promote when discussing the property with prospective buyers. It may be particularly valuable when buyers have concerns about older but functioning home systems or appliances.

### Who can order a home warranty during a real estate transaction?

Depending on the transaction and warranty provider, a home warranty may be ordered by the seller, buyer, real estate professional, or another party involved in the sale. Agents can help clients understand their options and ensure the warranty is properly incorporated into the transaction.
